# Resume Writing Guidelines & Strategy Rules

This rulebook is not just about formatting bullets. It is about making the resume sound like a real, senior, high-value engineer whose work reflects ownership, judgment, and technical depth without sounding stuffed, forced, or AI-generated.

The model must optimize for all 3 goals together:
1. ATS match
2. Senior-level credibility
3. Natural human sounding writing

If a rule improves ATS but makes the resume sound fake, forced, or inflated, do not follow it blindly. Always preserve realism and role-fit.

---

## 1. BULLET POINT LENGTH DEFINITIONS

Long: 230 to 280 characters  
Medium: 170 to 220 characters  
Short: 120 to 150 characters  

Bullet lengths should vary naturally. If every bullet has the same length, the resume looks templated and artificial.

---

## 2. LENGTH DISTRIBUTION BY JOB TYPE

The resume should vary its depth based on job type, not years.

### Fulltime Roles
Use a balanced structure with moderate technical depth and natural readability.

| Section | Bullets | Long | Medium | Short |
|--------|--------|------|--------|-------|
| Summary | 8 | 30% | 70% | 0% |
| Bee Data | 12 | 20% | 70% | 10% |
| Allied Health | 10 | 20% | 70% | 10% |
| BYJU'S | 9 | 20% | 60% | 20% |
| Cognizant | 7 to 8 | 20% | 50% | 30% |

### Contract Roles
Use broader and deeper technical framing. Contract roles should sound more implementation-heavy, system-facing, and technically explicit.

| Section | Bullets | Long | Medium | Short |
|--------|--------|------|--------|-------|
| Summary | 18 | 30% | 60% | 10% |
| Bee Data | 17 | 20% | 70% | 10% |
| Allied Health | 14 | 20% | 70% | 10% |
| BYJU'S | 11 | 20% | 60% | 20% |
| Cognizant | 10 | 20% | 50% | 30% |

Intent:
Fulltime resumes should feel stable, mature, and role-shaped.  
Contract resumes can show more breadth, deeper implementation detail, and wider ownership because contract work is often judged by delivery depth and technical specificity.

---

## 3. WRITING STYLE RULES

### Structure Mix
Experience bullets must not all follow the same structure.

Use this mix:
- 50% of experience bullets: problem or ownership + tool or system + result
- 30% of experience bullets: action + tool or system
- 20% of experience bullets: leadership, collaboration, architecture, review, mentoring, or process ownership

Intent:
A resume where every bullet is written in the same formula sounds generated. Variety creates realism.  
The preferred structure is important, but it should only shape about half the bullets, not all of them.

### Buffing Limits
At most 1 heavily buffed bullet per section.

Limits:
- Max 3 uses of "and" in one bullet
- Max 1 to 2 actions in a bullet
- Max 1 to 2 process clauses
- Max 1 clear outcome or effect

Intent:
Do not overpack a bullet with too many verbs, tools, and claims. Dense bullets look fake and are harder to scan.

### Grammar Rules
- Use only periods and commas
- Do not use semicolons
- Do not use a comma before "and"
- Keep the writing simple, direct, and easy to read
- Grammar does not need to be overly polished if it starts sounding robotic

Intent:
Natural readability matters more than sounding overly literary.

---

## 4. TECHNICAL DEPTH BY JOB TYPE

### Fulltime Roles
- 60% of bullets should show partial technical depth
- 40% can stay at system, architecture, or business-impact level
- Focus on tools, systems, purpose, and decision-making
- Avoid ground-level theory unless the JD strongly demands it

### Contract Roles
- 70% of bullets should show deeper technical depth
- 30% can stay high-level
- More implementation detail is allowed
- More explicit system logic, architecture tradeoffs, data flow, evaluation logic, and technical rationale are allowed

### Ground-Level Details Rule
Mention ground-level details such as:
- t-test
- chi-square
- loss functions
- statistical tests
- training-level model mechanics
- optimizer-level details

Only in Contract resumes, or when the JD explicitly demands that level of detail.

Do not force such details in Fulltime resumes because they often make the writing feel academic, junior, or overly granular.

Intent:
Contract resumes can go lower into system mechanics. Fulltime resumes should usually stay one layer higher unless the role is deeply research or modeling focused.

---

## 5. ROLE-SPECIFIC FOCUS

These are directional ownership areas, not a checklist to force into every resume.  
Read the JD first and choose what is most aligned.

### Data Scientist
Focus on:
- data infrastructure
- experimentation
- predictive modeling
- feature engineering
- evaluation rigor
- business translation
- production monitoring of models

### AI Engineer
Focus on:
- LLM systems
- RAG
- agentic workflows
- prompt engineering
- evaluation frameworks
- inference pipelines
- MLOps
- secure deployment
- platform systems
- Python-first backend ownership

### Software Engineer
Focus on:
- microservices
- APIs
- infrastructure
- event-driven systems
- Kubernetes
- automation
- scalable production services
- reliability

Intent:
The same background can be framed differently depending on target role.  
Do not rewrite history. Reframe emphasis.

---

## 6. PROJECT ADAPTATION BY ROLE

If the same project appears under different role targets, keep the project outcome consistent but change the narrative emphasis.

Examples:
- Data Scientist version should emphasize experimentation, features, analysis, model behavior, evaluation, and business interpretation
- AI Engineer version should emphasize LLM integration, RAG design, orchestration, deployment, evaluation, and inference systems
- Software Engineer version should emphasize services, APIs, scaling, infra, reliability, and production architecture

Intent:
Same project, different angle.  
Do not make every project sound identical across roles.

---

## 7. DESIGN VS IMPLEMENTATION BALANCE

Design work should appear in the resume as real system or project design ownership, not just as a fancy verb choice.

Examples of design ownership:
- defining architecture
- choosing serving pattern
- structuring ingestion or retrieval flow
- deciding deployment design
- selecting evaluation framework
- shaping monitoring strategy
- designing APIs or service boundaries

Do not treat "Designed" or "Architected" as cosmetic words. Use them only when the bullet shows real technical design judgment.

Intent:
The resume must show that the candidate did not only execute tasks. It must also show system thinking and design ownership.

---

## 8. GENAI AND LLM PLACEMENT RULES

- AI-related content should appear only in Bee Data and Allied Health
- ML-related content should appear in BYJU'S
- Software-focused content should appear in Cognizant
- For Data Scientist or Software Engineer targeting, include about 2 AI-oriented bullets across Bee Data and Allied Health
- These AI bullets should still be specific and credible

When mentioning AI work, prefer:
- integration
- system design
- evaluation
- deployment
- retrieval logic
- inference flow
- monitoring

Avoid vague phrases like:
- worked on GenAI
- used LLMs
- evaluated AI outputs

Intent:
Be specific. AI content must sound like real ownership, not hype.

---

## 9. SUMMARY SECTION RULES

The Summary should describe:
- expertise
- scope
- technical domain
- type of systems worked on
- level of ownership

Do not include quantified achievements in the Summary.

Never write things like:
- built an app used by 50,000+ users
- improved accuracy by 35%
- reduced latency by 40%

Metrics belong in Experience bullets where there is context.

Intent:
A quantified Summary sounds forced, salesy, and unprofessional.  
The Summary should establish profile quality, not try to prove impact with numbers.

---

## 10. FIRST BULLET RULE

The first bullet of every role must show one of the following:
- ownership
- system scope
- architecture responsibility
- business-critical responsibility
- platform impact

Bad:
Built ML model for prediction.

Good:
Owned development of a production-grade prediction workflow supporting lead prioritization across high-volume sales operations.

Intent:
Recruiters decide relevance quickly. The first bullet should immediately signal level, scope, and seriousness of work.

---

## 11. BULLET QUALITY RULE

Avoid tool-only bullets.

Bad:
Worked with Python and AWS.

Bad:
Used SageMaker for deployment.

Good:
Built and operationalized batch inference workflows in SageMaker to support recurring prediction jobs across time-sensitive business pipelines.

At least 50% of experience bullets should show:
problem or ownership + system or tool + business or operational effect

The remaining bullets can vary naturally across architecture, process, collaboration, debugging, mentoring, review, or delivery.

Intent:
Not every bullet should use the same pattern, but enough bullets must show clear depth and usefulness.

---

## 12. DOMAIN CONTEXT RULE

Keep business context natural. Do not force domain language into every bullet if Claude is already writing it well.

Only include domain context when it helps clarify:
- what system was built
- who used it
- why it mattered
- how it connected to operations

Intent:
Business context should support realism, not make the resume sound repetitive or overexplained.

---

## 13. TOOL DENSITY RULE

Do not overload a bullet with tools.

Rule:
Maximum 2 major tools or platforms per bullet in most cases.

Only exceed this when the architecture truly requires it and the sentence still reads naturally.

Bad:
Built pipelines using Python, SQL, AWS, SageMaker, Docker, Kubernetes, MLflow, Airflow, and Snowflake.

Intent:
Too many tools in one bullet looks like keyword stuffing and lowers credibility.

---

## 14. METRICS AND NUMBERS

- Around 70% of bullets in each work experience should include some measurable signal
- Around 60% should include quantified results
- Remaining bullets can show effect without exact numbers

Metrics should appear where they feel natural:
- latency
- scale
- throughput
- volume
- automation %
- accuracy
- cost reduction
- processing time
- usage scale
- delivery cadence

Do not force metrics into every bullet.

Intent:
Metrics strengthen credibility only when they fit naturally.

---

## 15. OWNERSHIP AREA RULE

Before writing any bullets, identify 5 to 7 Core Technical Ownership Areas from the JD.

These are not tools.  
These are the real decisions and responsibilities the role owns end to end.

Examples:
- inference platform design
- retrieval architecture
- CI/CD for ML services
- model deployment and monitoring
- API service ownership
- data quality enforcement
- evaluation strategy
- secure cloud deployment

Rule:
At least 60% of experience bullets should map to one of these ownership areas.

Before accepting a bullet, test it:
Does this bullet show a responsibility owned, a decision made, or a system shaped  
or does it only mention a tool?

Intent:
This step must happen before writing bullets, not after.  
It is the backbone of role-fit and seniority.

---

## 16. ATS KEYWORD STRATEGY

Extract strong phrases directly from the JD and embed them naturally.

If the JD says:
- batch inference pipelines
- model deployment
- vector databases
- production APIs
- CI/CD for ML
- model monitoring

Use the same phrase or a very close variant in the resume.

Placement priority:
1. Skills section
2. First 3 bullets of latest role
3. Relevant bullets in older roles
4. Projects if needed

Intent:
Important ATS keywords have more value when they appear in the most visible sections, not buried late in the resume.

---

## 17. VOCABULARY VARIATION RULE

Within a section, do not repeat the same phrase root more than once unless truly necessary.

Examples of phrase roots:
- ML pipeline
- ML workflow
- ML training
- data pipeline
- inference pipeline

Rotate wording naturally:
- pipeline
- workflow
- service
- framework
- system
- deployment path
- serving layer
- orchestration flow

Intent:
Repetition makes writing sound templated and weakens the impression of depth.

---

## 18. SKILLS SECTION ORDERING RULE

The order of skill categories must follow JD priority, not a fixed template.

If the JD emphasizes cloud and deployment first, lead with that.  
If it emphasizes LLMs and orchestration first, lead with those.

Intent:
Skills order itself signals alignment.

---

## 19. NATURAL TECHNOLOGY SELECTION RULE

Technologies in a JD are usually OR conditions, not AND conditions.

Choose naturally.

### Cloud
If JD says cloud experience:
- Prefer AWS as primary
- Mention GCP or Azure only if useful and credible
- Do not present all clouds equally unless the background truly supports it

### BI Tools
Choose one primary tool naturally, not all

### ML Frameworks
Prefer one primary framework and mention another only if justified

### Databases
Mention only the few that realistically fit the role and work history

Intent:
The resume must sound like a real engineer with practical tool depth, not a checklist.

---

## 20. STARTUP VS ENTERPRISE RULE

### Startup
Show wider breadth, faster ownership, broader stack exposure, and adaptability.

### Enterprise
Stay tighter to the JD. Show depth, process maturity, reliability, and role-specific ownership.

Intent:
The same person should not sound the same for both environments.

---

## 21. SENIOR SIGNAL RULE

For mid-level and senior AI or engineering roles, every major experience should include at least one bullet that signals one or more of the following:
- architecture ownership
- performance optimization
- scalability
- monitoring or reliability
- deployment maturity
- technical review or mentoring

Intent:
A senior engineer should not sound like someone who only executed tasks.  
The resume must show operational maturity and system responsibility.

---

## 22. SKILLS CATEGORY RULE FOR SENIOR AI ENGINEER

Avoid generic, weak, or low-signal headings such as:
- Monitoring
- Version Control
- Production AI
- Python Core
- Statistical Analysis
- Data Warehousing
- ETL & Pipelines
- business communication
- c-suite meetings

For senior AI or AI platform roles, prefer stronger categories such as:
- Programming
- AI Frameworks
- GenAI Technologies
- Cloud Platforms
- Container Platforms
- Data Infrastructure
- APIs and Backend Services
- Vector and Retrieval Systems
- Model Operations
- Databases

Intent:
Skill categories should sound like real technical domains, not generic resume filler.

---

## 23. FORBIDDEN ELEMENTS

Never use:
- arrows
- decorative symbols
- visual gimmicks
- special characters that reduce ATS readability

Use:
- % symbol instead of writing percent

Parentheses:
- allowed only when useful for subtopics
- keep them minimal
- do not overuse bracketed stacks of tools

Intent:
Formatting should stay ATS-safe and visually professional.

---

## 24. STYLE BENCHMARK RULE

Use the DA ML style resume as a benchmark for:
- line density
- tone
- versatility
- high-level impression
- bullet maturity

Do not copy it blindly.  
Use it as a calibration reference for how a strong, versatile, high-level resume should feel.

Intent:
The target is not only keyword match. It is also strong professional texture.

---

## 25. FINAL REALISM TEST

Before finalizing the resume, check:

1. Does it sound like a real engineer, not an AI-generated keyword sheet  
2. Does the latest role immediately show ownership and relevance  
3. Do the bullets vary in structure and rhythm  
4. Are metrics placed naturally inside Experience, not Summary  
5. Does the resume reflect the target role without forcing every tool from the JD  
6. Do the skill categories sound senior and technically credible  
7. Would a hiring manager believe this person actually did the work described

If any answer is no, revise before finalizing.
